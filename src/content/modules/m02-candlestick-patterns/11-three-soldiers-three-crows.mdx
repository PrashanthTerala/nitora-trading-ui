One strong candle can be a fluke. One big order, one news headline, one nervous fund manager. Two in a row is more interesting. Three strong candles in a row, each starting inside the last and closing near its high, is a crowd that has made up its mind and is acting on it, day after day.

That is the picture behind **three white soldiers** and **three black crows**. Soldiers are three tall green candles marching upward after a decline. Crows are three tall red candles dropping lower after a rise. The names come from old Japanese charts, where up candles were drawn white and down candles black.

These are momentum patterns, not rejection patterns. There are no long wicks, no stalls, no hesitation. That makes them easy to read and, oddly, tricky to trade, because by the time three tall candles have printed, a lot of the move has already happened.

## Three white soldiers: exact rules

- The market has been in a **downtrend** or a sideways period; the soldiers appear as a reversal or as the start of a new leg up.
- **Three consecutive green candles**, each with a **large body** and **small upper wicks** (each closes at or near its high).
- Each candle **opens inside the body of the previous candle**, usually in the upper half, and **closes above the previous close**, making a new high each session.
- The bodies should be **roughly similar in size**. A soldier that is much smaller than the previous one, or that has a long upper wick, is a warning that the advance is tiring.

### The psychology

Day one: buyers step in after the decline and close strong. Day two opens a little lower, inside yesterday's body, which gives sellers a chance, and buyers absorb it and close at a new high. Day three, the same. Three times in a row, any dip has been bought and the session has finished near the top. There is no sign of sellers being able to hold price down for even part of a session.

This is what orderly demand looks like. Nobody is panicking in; each day opens with a small pullback and finishes strong. That orderliness is why the pattern is respected, and why a disorderly version, with huge candles and gaps, is treated differently.

<Callout type="eli5">

Picture three friends carrying a heavy box up a staircase. Each one takes a step, pauses to grip, then takes another. No slipping, no dropping it. After three clean steps, everyone watching believes the box is going up. That is three white soldiers.

</Callout>

## Three black crows: the mirror

- The market has been in an **uptrend**.
- **Three consecutive red candles**, each with a large body and small lower wicks (each closes at or near its low).
- Each candle **opens inside the body of the previous candle** and **closes below the previous close**, making a new low each session.

<PatternFigure name="three-black-crows" caption="Three tall red candles, each opening inside the previous body and closing near its low. Every bounce at the open is sold, and every session ends near the bottom." />

The story is orderly supply. Each day opens with a small bounce, sellers absorb it, and the close is near the low. Three days of that after a rally means the buyers who drove the rally are being replaced by sellers who are not in a hurry but are not stopping either.

## Variations worth knowing

**Advance block.** Three green candles where each body is smaller than the last and upper wicks grow longer. The soldiers are tiring. This is a warning that the advance is losing steam, not a bullish signal, despite being three green candles.

**Stalled pattern (deliberation).** Two strong green candles followed by a third with a small body that sits at the top, like a spinning top perched on the second candle. Momentum has stalled at the high. Also a warning.

**Identical three crows.** Three black crows where each candle opens at or very near the previous close, with almost no bounce. That shows even more urgency from sellers and is considered a stronger, though rarer, version.

The lesson from all three variations: the pattern is about *quality* of movement. Three green candles are not automatically soldiers. Look at whether the bodies are tall, whether the wicks are small and whether each session finished strong.

## Where they are valid

Soldiers are most meaningful when they follow a decline or a long consolidation and break out of it. Crows are most meaningful when they follow an extended rise, especially one that had already shown signs of tiring (spinning tops, a shooting star, a bearish engulfing that did not follow through).

Both patterns are also useful as continuation confirmations. Three soldiers in the middle of an established uptrend, after a pullback, say the trend is healthy. Three crows in a downtrend, after a bounce, say the same in reverse.

Where they are least useful is after an already extended move in the same direction. Three giant soldiers at the end of a month-long rally may be the final blow-off, where the last buyers pile in. The next day's candle will tell you.

## Confirmation

Because the pattern already contains three sessions of agreement, many traders treat the third candle as confirmation in itself. A cautious approach waits for a fourth candle that does not immediately give back a large part of the third candle. Volume that stays steady or rises across the three sessions supports the pattern; volume that shrinks each day suggests the move is running out of participants.

## A trade idea to practise

This is where soldiers and crows get awkward. If you buy at the close of the third soldier, your natural stop, below the low of the first soldier, is far away, and the move you were hoping for may be partly done. Two practical answers:

1. **Wait for a pullback.** After three soldiers, price often retraces into the body of the third or second candle. Enter on that pullback with a stop below the low of the third soldier.
2. **Take a smaller position** with the stop below the first soldier's low, accepting the wide stop because the pattern is strong.

For three black crows, the mirror: short on a bounce into the body of the third crow, stop above its high.

<Callout type="warning" title="Chasing the third candle">

The urge to buy the third soldier at its close, because it looks so obviously strong, is exactly the FOMO that the psychology module will warn you about. Strong-looking entries are often the worst ones because everyone else is seeing the same thing and buying the same close. The pattern is a reason to be bullish. The pullback is the entry.

</Callout>

## Honest reliability

Three white soldiers and three black crows are among the best performers in Bulkowski's stock data. Three black crows reverses the uptrend around 78% of the time, and three white soldiers reverses the downtrend around 82%. Those are unusually high for candlesticks.

But be careful with those numbers. Because the pattern takes three strong sessions, some of the "reversal" has already happened by the time you can name it, and the follow-through after the pattern is often modest. Bulkowski himself rates the pattern's performance after the breakout as only moderate. So the honest summary is: three soldiers or crows are very good at telling you which way the market has turned, and only fair at telling you how much further it goes.
