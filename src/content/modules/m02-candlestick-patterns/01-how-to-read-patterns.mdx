Imagine a photo of two kids in a tug of war. In the picture, one team is leaning back, the other is sliding forward, and the rope is off-centre. You can tell a lot from that one frame: who is winning right now, who is tired, who just lost their footing. What you cannot tell is who will win. A photo is a moment, not a prophecy.

A **candlestick pattern** is exactly that kind of photo. It is one, two or three candles that capture a moment in the fight between buyers and sellers. This module teaches you the whole vocabulary of those moments: dojis, hammers, engulfing candles, stars, soldiers and crows. Before you learn a single shape, though, you need the frame of mind that makes them useful instead of dangerous.

That frame of mind has three parts: patterns are psychology snapshots, context beats shape, and confirmation is the price of admission. Get these three right and every lesson that follows will click into place. Skip them and you will become a pattern hunter, which is the fastest way to lose money with a chart.

## A pattern is a snapshot of psychology

In [Anatomy of a Candlestick](/learn/m01-reading-price/03-anatomy-of-a-candle) you learned that every candle is a battle report: the <Term id="body">body</Term> shows where the fight started and ended, and the <Term id="wick">wicks</Term> show how far each side pushed before being shoved back. A pattern is simply a short sequence of those reports that tells a recognisable story.

Take the most famous one, the <Term id="hammer">hammer</Term>. Price opens, sellers drive it down hard, and then buyers storm back so completely that the candle closes near where it opened. The long lower wick is the scar from the sellers' attack. The tiny body at the top is proof that the attack failed.

<PatternFigure name="hammer" caption="A hammer after a decline: sellers pushed price far down during the session, buyers took every bit of it back. The next candle closing higher is the confirmation." />

Notice what the hammer does and does not tell you. It tells you that, in this one session, sellers lost a fight they should have won. It does not tell you the downtrend is over. The sellers might come back tomorrow with more friends. That is why the picture above includes the next candle, and why the label on it says confirmation.

<Callout type="eli5">

Think of a candle pattern like the score at half-time in a football match. If the home team was losing 0 to 3 and just scored twice, the mood in the stadium has changed. But the match is not over, and nobody should bet the house on the half-time score. A pattern tells you the mood has changed, not who wins.

</Callout>

## Context beats shape

Here is the rule that separates people who use patterns well from people who lose money with them: **the same shape means different things in different places.** A hammer is only a hammer if there was a decline to reverse. Put the identical candle in the middle of a sideways chop and it is just a candle with a long wick.

<PatternFigure name="hammer-no-context" caption="Same shape, no trend behind it. There is nothing to reverse, so there is nothing to trade. Most beginner pattern losses come from this picture." />

Context has three layers, and you will check all three before trusting any pattern in this module.

**Trend.** Reversal patterns need a <Term id="trend">trend</Term> to reverse. Bullish reversals (hammer, bullish engulfing, morning star) require a prior decline. Bearish reversals (shooting star, bearish engulfing, evening star) require a prior rise. Continuation patterns require an existing trend in the direction they continue. No trend, no pattern.

**Location.** Patterns that form at a level the market has respected before, a <Term id="support">support</Term> or <Term id="resistance">resistance</Term> zone, are worth far more than patterns floating in the middle of nowhere. A hammer at a support level that has held twice is two reasons stacked on top of each other.

<PatternFigure name="pattern-at-support" caption="The hammer forms exactly where price bounced before. Trend to reverse, plus a level to bounce from: this is what a real signal looks like." />

**Timeframe.** A hammer on a daily chart summarises a whole day of fighting and carries more weight than a hammer on a one-minute chart that summarises sixty seconds. Neither is magic, but the higher the [timeframe](/learn/m01-reading-price/05-timeframes), the more traders saw and reacted to the same candle, which is part of why patterns work at all.

## Confirmation is the price of admission

Every pattern in this module has a **confirmation** rule, and you will see the word <Term id="confirmation">confirmation</Term> in every lesson. It means: do not act on the pattern candle itself. Wait for the next candle (or the one after) to close in the direction the pattern predicts. For a hammer, that means the next candle closes above the hammer's close. For a shooting star, the next candle closes below the star's close.

Why wait? Because the pattern candle is the half-time score. The confirmation candle is the first minutes of the second half, and it tells you whether the mood change was real or whether the losing team just got lucky once. Waiting costs you a slightly worse entry price. Not waiting costs you a lot of trades that never went anywhere.

Traders confirm patterns in three main ways, and stronger signals tick more than one box:

| Confirmation | What you are looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Next candle close | A close beyond the pattern in the predicted direction |
| Volume | Higher [volume](/learn/m01-reading-price/06-volume) on the pattern or confirmation candle than on the candles before it |
| Level | The pattern sits at a support or resistance zone that has held before |

<Callout type="warning" title="The pattern-hunting trap">

Once you know twenty patterns, you will see them everywhere. Your brain is a pattern-matching machine and it will happily find a hammer in every long wick and a doji in every small candle. The cure is boring: only count a pattern when the trend, the location and the confirmation are all present. If you find yourself squinting to make a shape fit, it does not fit.

</Callout>

## The honest odds

Thomas Bulkowski tested thousands of these patterns on real stock data and published the results. The short version: most candlestick patterns, taken on their own, predict the next move correctly somewhere between 50% and 65% of the time. A few are worse than a coin flip, and a handful of three-candle patterns reach the 70s. Lesson 16 gives you the table.

That sounds disappointing until you remember that a coin flip with a small edge, combined with good risk management, is how most professional trading works. A pattern that is right 58% of the time at a support level with confirmation, where you risk $1 to make $2, is a real business. A pattern that is right 58% of the time in the middle of nowhere with no confirmation, where you risk $2 to make $1, is a slow way to go broke.

So the goal of this module is not to memorise shapes. The goal is to learn what each shape says about the crowd, where it is allowed to appear, what counts as confirmation, and roughly how much to trust it. Every lesson follows that same recipe.

## How each lesson is laid out

For every pattern you will get the same six things. First, **what it looks like**, with exact rules about body size, wick ratios and where the candle must sit. Second, **the psychology**: who won the battle and why. Third, **where it is valid**: the trend context it needs. Fourth, **how traders confirm it**. Fifth, **a typical entry and stop**, as an idea to practise, not a recommendation. Sixth, **honest reliability notes**.

You will also be sent to the pattern trainer, which shows you random candle snippets and asks you to name the pattern. Use it. Recognition should become automatic so that your thinking time goes to context and confirmation, where it belongs.

<Callout type="tip">

A good habit from day one: when you spot a pattern, say out loud what the sellers and the buyers did in that candle. If you cannot tell the story, you do not understand the pattern yet, and you should not trade it.

</Callout>

<KeyTakeaways>
- A candlestick pattern is a snapshot of who won a short fight between buyers and sellers, not a prediction.
- The same shape means different things in different places: reversal patterns need a trend to reverse.
- Patterns at support or resistance, on higher timeframes, are worth far more than patterns in the middle of nowhere.
- Never act on the pattern candle itself; wait for the next candle to close in the predicted direction.
- Most patterns are right 50% to 65% of the time on their own; the edge comes from context, confirmation and risk management.
- Learn the psychology behind each shape so you can tell its story, not just recognise it.
</KeyTakeaways>
