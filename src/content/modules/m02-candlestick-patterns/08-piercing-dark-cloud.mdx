In a tug of war, the rope has a mark in the middle. If your team has been dragged five steps back and then pulls the rope three steps forward, the crowd cheers, but the mark is still on their side. If you pull it six steps forward, past the middle, the mood in the whole field changes. Halfway is the line that matters.

Candlesticks have a halfway rule too. The **piercing line** and the **dark cloud cover** are two-candle reversal patterns where the second candle claws back more than half of the first candle's body, but does not fully engulf it. They are the engulfing pattern's less dramatic cousins: a strong counterattack that gets past the middle of the rope but not all the way.

The 50% rule is the whole pattern. Learn to eyeball the midpoint of a candle body and you will never confuse a piercing line with the weaker patterns that look like it.

## The piercing line: exact rules

- The market is in a **downtrend**.
- **Candle one** is a large red candle. The bigger, the better.
- **Candle two opens below candle one's low** (a gap down at the open, the classic rule; many traders relax this to opening below candle one's close).
- Candle two is green and **closes above the midpoint of candle one's body**, but **below candle one's open**. If it closed above the open, it would be a bullish engulfing instead.

<PatternFigure name="piercing-line" caption="After a decline, a big red candle is followed by a green candle that opens below the red low and closes above the midpoint of the red body. The line marks the 50% level the close must beat." />

### The psychology

Day one is a solid win for the sellers. Day two opens even lower, which is the sellers' dream: panic is spreading. Then buyers step in and push relentlessly. By the close, more than half of yesterday's losses have been recovered.

Think about who is hurt. Everyone who sold at day two's open is losing. Everyone who sold in the bottom half of day one's body is losing. The sellers had a gift, a gap down, and squandered it. That is a serious blow to seller confidence, and it is why the piercing line is a **bullish reversal** candidate.

<Callout type="eli5">

Imagine a snowball fight where one team has been winning all afternoon. Then the other team lands one big throw that knocks their opponents back more than halfway to the starting line. It is not a total rout, but the confident team is suddenly not so confident.

</Callout>

## Dark cloud cover: the mirror

- The market is in an **uptrend**.
- **Candle one** is a large green candle.
- **Candle two opens above candle one's high** (a gap up; relaxed versions allow opening above candle one's close).
- Candle two is red and **closes below the midpoint of candle one's body**, but **above candle one's open**.

<PatternFigure name="dark-cloud-cover" caption="At the top of a rise, a big green candle is followed by a red candle that opens above the green high and closes below the midpoint of the green body. A dark cloud rolling over a sunny rally." />

The name is exactly the picture: a dark cloud settling over a sunny day. The rally gapped up, pulled in the last excited buyers at the open, and then sellers drove price back through more than half of yesterday's gains. Late buyers are trapped above the close, and the momentum that carried the rally has been visibly broken.

## Why 50% matters

The midpoint rule is not arbitrary. If the second candle recovers less than half of the first candle's body, the first candle's side still "owns" more of the range. That weaker version has its own names, thrusting line and in-neck line, and those are continuation patterns more often than not. You will meet them in lesson 15.

So the same two-candle shape sits on a scale:

| Second candle closes... | Name | Usual meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At or just below the first candle's low | On-neck | Continuation |
| Just inside the first candle's body | In-neck | Continuation |
| Into the body but below the midpoint | Thrusting | Continuation, weak |
| Above the midpoint, below the open | Piercing line | Reversal |
| Above the first candle's open | Bullish engulfing | Reversal, stronger |

The further the second candle reaches, the more the story changes from "weak bounce" to "real counterattack". The 50% line is where the change happens. The deeper the piercing, the stronger the signal: a close at 80% of the body is far better than one at 51%.

## Where it is valid

Both patterns are reversal patterns and need a clear <Term id="trend">trend</Term> to reverse. The piercing line is most reliable after an extended decline into a <Term id="support">support</Term> level. Dark cloud cover is most reliable after an extended rise into a <Term id="resistance">resistance</Term> level, especially when the gap up at candle two's open pokes above the level and the close falls back beneath it.

The gap at the open is more common in stock markets that close overnight than in 24-hour markets like forex or crypto, where the next candle opens exactly where the last one closed. In those markets, use the relaxed rule: candle two opens at or below the prior close and still recovers the midpoint.

## Confirmation

For a piercing line, confirmation is a third candle closing above the first candle's open, which is also the level where the pattern would have become an engulfing. For dark cloud cover, a third candle closing below the first candle's open. Higher [volume](/learn/m01-reading-price/06-volume) on the second candle adds weight: a piercing line on heavy volume means a lot of shares changed hands at the low.

## A trade idea to practise

Piercing line: enter long when the confirmation candle closes above the first candle's open. Stop below the low of the second candle, which is the low of the pattern and the level buyers defended. Target the next resistance.

Dark cloud cover: enter short when the confirmation candle closes below the first candle's open. Stop above the high of the second candle. Target the next support.

<Callout type="warning" title="Measure, do not guess">

The difference between a piercing line and a thrusting line is a close a few cents above or below the midpoint. That small difference flips the pattern from reversal to continuation. Look at the actual numbers: open, close, midpoint. If the close is not clearly above the midpoint, do not call it a piercing line. The trainer is good for this because it forces you to look at the body proportions.

</Callout>

## Honest reliability

Bulkowski's numbers are modest. The piercing line reverses the downtrend about 64% of the time in his stock data, which is respectable. Dark cloud cover is weaker, closer to 60%, and some studies put it barely above a coin flip. Both are less reliable than their engulfing cousins, which fits the story: a counterattack that stops short of the open leaves some of the enemy standing.

Practical version: treat a confirmed piercing line at support as a decent signal, treat dark cloud cover at resistance as a moderate one, and treat either in open space with no confirmation as noise.

- Piercing line: after a decline, a big red candle, then a green candle that opens below its low and closes above its midpoint but below its open.
- Dark cloud cover: after a rise, a big green candle, then a red candle that opens above its high and closes below its midpoint but above its open.
- The 50% rule is the whole pattern; less than halfway is a weak bounce (thrusting, in-neck) that usually continues the trend.
- The deeper the second candle reaches past the midpoint, the stronger the signal.
- Confirm with a third candle closing beyond the first candle's open; stop beyond the second candle's extreme.
- Piercing line tests around 64%; dark cloud cover nearer 60% and sometimes barely above a coin flip.
</KeyTakeaways>
