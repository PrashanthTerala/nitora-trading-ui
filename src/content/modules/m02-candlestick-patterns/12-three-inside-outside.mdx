In the last few lessons you kept hearing the same instruction: wait for the next candle to close in the predicted direction. The harami needs it badly. The engulfing pattern is better with it. Traders got so used to adding that confirmation candle that they gave the confirmed versions their own names.

That is all this lesson is. **Three inside up** is a bullish harami plus a confirming third candle. **Three inside down** is a bearish harami plus a confirming third candle. **Three outside up** is a bullish engulfing plus confirmation. **Three outside down** is a bearish engulfing plus confirmation. "Inside" refers to the harami's small candle sitting inside the big one; "outside" refers to the engulfing candle wrapping around the previous one.

If you already understand the [harami](/learn/m02-candlestick-patterns/07-harami-and-harami-cross) and the [engulfing pattern](/learn/m02-candlestick-patterns/06-engulfing), you already understand these four. The new idea is what the third candle must do to count, and what the statistics say about adding it.

## Three inside up: exact rules

- The market is in a **downtrend**.
- **Candles one and two** form a **bullish harami**: a big red candle, then a small candle whose body sits inside the red body.
- **Candle three** is green and **closes above candle one's open**, which is the top of the harami's big candle. Some traders relax this to a close above candle two's high, but the classic rule is the stricter one.

<PatternFigure name="three-inside-up" caption="A bullish harami (big red, small inside) followed by a green candle closing above the first candle's open. The stall has resolved upward; the harami is confirmed." />

The psychology is the harami's psychology, completed. Day one, sellers win big. Day two, they stall. Day three, buyers do not just nudge price up; they push it above where the big red candle began, which means every seller from day one is now losing. The stall has become a turn.

<Callout type="eli5">

Remember the shopping trolley that hit thick grass and slowed down? Three inside up is the trolley slowing (day two) and then rolling back up the slope past where it started (day three). Now you know which way the grass sent it.

</Callout>

## Three inside down

- The market is in an **uptrend**.
- **Candles one and two** form a **bearish harami**: big green, then a small candle inside it.
- **Candle three** is red and **closes below candle one's open**.

<PatternFigure name="three-inside-down" caption="A bearish harami followed by a red candle closing below the first candle's open. Buyers stalled on day two and were overrun on day three." />

## Three outside up: exact rules

- The market is in a **downtrend**.
- **Candles one and two** form a **bullish engulfing**: a small red candle, then a green candle whose body fully covers it.
- **Candle three** is green and **closes higher than candle two's close**.

<PatternFigure name="three-outside-up" caption="A bullish engulfing followed by another green candle closing higher. The engulfing was not a one-day wonder; buyers kept pushing." />

The engulfing already showed buyers taking control. The third candle shows they kept it. The difference matters because a single big green candle can be one large order; two closes in a row in the same direction are harder to explain away.

## Three outside down

- The market is in an **uptrend**.
- **Candles one and two** form a **bearish engulfing**.
- **Candle three** is red and **closes lower than candle two's close**.

<PatternFigure name="three-outside-down" caption="A bearish engulfing followed by another red candle closing lower. Sellers took control on day two and kept it on day three." />

## What the third candle must do

The exact rule for candle three differs between the inside and outside versions, and it is worth being precise:

| Pattern | Two-candle base | Third candle must close... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three inside up | Bullish harami | Above the first candle's open |
| Three inside down | Bearish harami | Below the first candle's open |
| Three outside up | Bullish engulfing | Above the second candle's close |
| Three outside down | Bearish engulfing | Below the second candle's close |

The inside versions have the harder test, because the harami's small candle started inside a big one, so the third candle has to travel further to prove the reversal. The outside versions only need "one more close in the same direction", because the engulfing already did most of the work.

## Where they are valid

Everything from the harami and engulfing lessons applies. These are reversal patterns and need a prior <Term id="trend">trend</Term>. They are strongest at <Term id="support">support</Term> (up versions) and <Term id="resistance">resistance</Term> (down versions), and stronger on daily charts than on intraday charts.

One extra point: because these patterns contain their own confirmation, they are often the versions you actually trade even if you spotted the harami or engulfing first. Seeing a harami tells you to watch; seeing three inside up tells you the watch is over.

## Confirmation and a trade idea

Since the third candle is the confirmation, most traders enter at its close or on the next open. A cautious trader waits for a fourth candle that holds above (or below) the third candle's midpoint.

Three inside up: enter at the third candle's close. Stop below the low of the big red candle, which is the pattern's low. Target the next resistance.

Three outside up: enter at the third candle's close. Stop below the low of the engulfing candle. Target the next resistance.

The down versions mirror these with stops above the pattern high. The usual warning about wide stops applies: three sessions of movement can put the stop a long way from entry, so size the position to the stop.

<Callout type="warning" title="Confirmation costs you price">

Waiting for the third candle means you enter later and at a worse price than a trader who bought the harami's small candle or the engulfing close. That is the trade-off: fewer false signals, but a smaller share of each move and a wider stop. There is no free lunch here. Confirmation buys accuracy with price, and you will have to decide, in your own testing, which you prefer.

</Callout>

## Honest reliability

Here is the interesting part. Adding the confirmation candle helps the harami a lot. Bulkowski's data has the plain bullish harami at about 53%, while three inside up comes in around 65% and three inside down around 60%. The stall-then-resolve structure removes many of the haramis that simply resumed the trend.

The outside versions barely improve on the engulfing patterns they contain. Three outside up tests around 75% and three outside down around 69%, roughly in line with or slightly below the engulfing figures, depending on the sample. That makes sense: the engulfing candle already contained a change of control, so one more close mostly confirms what was already likely.

The practical lesson: confirmation is worth the most when the base pattern is a stall (harami, doji, spinning top), and worth the least when the base pattern is already a decisive shift (engulfing, marubozu). Spend your patience where it pays.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Three inside up/down is a harami plus a third candle closing beyond the first candle's open.
- Three outside up/down is an engulfing plus a third candle closing beyond the second candle's close.
- The third candle turns a stall (harami) or a shift (engulfing) into a confirmed reversal.
- They need a prior trend and work best at support or resistance, on daily charts.
- Enter at the third candle's close; stop beyond the pattern's extreme; size to the wider stop.
- Confirmation lifts the harami from about 53% to the low 60s; it barely changes the engulfing's odds.
</KeyTakeaways>
