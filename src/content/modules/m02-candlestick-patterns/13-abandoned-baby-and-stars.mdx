Sometimes the market does not just pause; it jumps. Price closes at one level, and the next session opens somewhere else entirely, leaving an empty space on the chart. You met these spaces in [Gaps](/learn/m01-reading-price/07-gaps). When a gap surrounds the middle candle of a star pattern on both sides, the pattern becomes something rarer and stronger.

This lesson covers the gapped stars: the **morning doji star** and **evening doji star**, where the middle candle is a doji, and the **abandoned baby**, where the doji is isolated by a gap on both sides. They are the elite versions of the [morning and evening star](/learn/m02-candlestick-patterns/10-morning-evening-star), and they show up perhaps a handful of times a year on any single daily chart.

Rarity cuts both ways. These patterns test well, but the samples are small, so treat their statistics as encouraging rather than proven.

## Morning doji star: exact rules

- The market is in a **downtrend**.
- **Candle one** is a large red candle.
- **Candle two** is a **doji** (body under roughly 5% to 10% of its range) whose body sits **below candle one's body**, classically after a gap down.
- **Candle three** is a large green candle closing well into candle one's body, past the midpoint.

<PatternFigure name="morning-doji-star" caption="A morning star whose middle candle is a doji. The stall in the middle session was total: sellers had the advantage and could not move price at all. Rarer than the plain morning star and considered stronger." />

The difference from an ordinary morning star is the depth of the stall. A spinning top in the middle says the sellers could not press their advantage. A doji says they could not move price at all: the session opened and closed at the same price. Since the pattern's whole meaning comes from the stall, a deeper stall makes a stronger pattern.

The **evening doji star** is the mirror: a big green candle, a doji perched above it, then a big red candle closing deep into the green body.

<Callout type="eli5">

Think of a car sliding on ice toward a wall. A morning star is the car slowing right down and then driving away. A morning doji star is the car coming to a dead stop, not even rolling, and then driving away. The dead stop is more convincing.

</Callout>

## The abandoned baby: exact rules

The abandoned baby adds one demanding condition to the doji star: **the doji must be gapped away from both neighbours, wicks included.** The doji's entire range, from its high to its low, sits below the low of candle one and below the low of candle three (bullish version). Nothing touches the baby.

- The market is in a **downtrend** (bullish abandoned baby) or **uptrend** (bearish).
- **Candle one** is a large candle in the direction of the trend.
- **Candle two** is a doji with a **gap on both sides**: its high is below the lows of both neighbours (bullish), or its low is above the highs of both neighbours (bearish).
- **Candle three** is a large candle in the reversal direction that closes well into candle one's body.

### The psychology

The gap down into the doji is panic: sellers were so eager that the open jumped below yesterday's entire range. Then, on the doji, nothing. All that eagerness produced no further movement. Then the market gaps back up above the doji's entire range. The price level where the doji traded has been abandoned, first by the sellers who could not push lower and then by the market that jumped away from it.

Anyone who sold at the doji's price is now stranded below a gap, losing money, with no way to get out at the same price. That is a trapped crowd, and trapped crowds fuel reversals.

## Where they are valid

All of these are reversal patterns and need a prior <Term id="trend">trend</Term>. They are far more common in markets that close overnight (stocks, index futures during cash hours) than in 24-hour markets like forex or crypto, where gaps mostly appear only over weekends. On a 24-hour chart, a true abandoned baby is nearly impossible; the closest you will get is a morning or evening doji star with no gaps, which is a weaker pattern.

They are also more meaningful on daily and weekly charts than on intraday charts, for the same reason: the gaps that isolate the baby come from overnight news and repricing.

A gapped star that forms at a <Term id="support">support</Term> level (bullish) or <Term id="resistance">resistance</Term> level (bearish) is the top of the candlestick hierarchy. When you see one, it deserves your full attention. That does not mean it always works; it means it is one of the few patterns where the shape alone is telling you something unusual happened.

## Confirmation and a trade idea

As with all star patterns, the third candle is a built-in confirmation. The pattern is not complete until candle three closes deep into candle one's body. Most traders enter at that close or the next open, because waiting for a fourth candle on a pattern this strong often means missing the move. A gap up on the third candle (bullish) or gap down (bearish) is extra evidence.

Bullish abandoned baby or morning doji star: enter at the third candle's close. Stop a small buffer below the doji's low. That level is the point the market abandoned; if price returns to it, the abandonment was false. Target the next resistance or a multiple of your risk.

Bearish version: enter short at the third candle's close. Stop above the doji's high.

<Callout type="danger" title="Gaps that fill are not gaps that held">

The abandoned baby's power comes from the gaps. If, on the third candle or the one after, price trades back down into the doji's range, the gap has filled and the pattern has failed even if the candles still look right. Do not hold a losing position on the argument that the shape was textbook. The stop under the doji's low exists exactly for this case.

</Callout>

## Honest reliability

The morning doji star reverses the downtrend around 76% of the time in Bulkowski's data, and the evening doji star around 71%, close to the plain stars. The bullish abandoned baby comes in around 70% and the bearish version around 69%. Those are good numbers by candlestick standards.

Two heavy caveats. First, these patterns are rare, so the sample sizes behind those percentages are small, and small samples wobble. Second, because they require gaps, they are effectively stock-market patterns; do not carry the numbers over to forex or crypto. The honest summary: gapped doji stars at a level are among the most trustworthy candle signals you will find, and you will find very few of them.

<KeyTakeaways>
- A morning or evening doji star is a star pattern whose middle candle is a doji: the stall was total, so the pattern is stronger.
- An abandoned baby is a doji star where the doji's whole range is gapped away from both neighbours.
- The gaps show panic, then nothing, then a jump back, leaving anyone who traded the doji stranded.
- These need overnight gaps, so they are stock and index patterns; they are rare and weaker on 24-hour charts.
- Enter at the third candle's close; stop just beyond the doji's extreme; a filled gap means the pattern has failed.
- Reliability tests around 70% to 76%, but samples are small and the patterns are rare.
</KeyTakeaways>
